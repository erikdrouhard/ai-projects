# Sales Safari — Method Reference

Sales Safari is a customer research technique created by **Amy Hoy** (Stacking the Bricks / 30x500 Academy). Instead of asking people what they want via surveys or interviews, you go where they already hang out online and observe what they're already saying about their problems. It's ethnographic research for product and business development.

## The Process

1. **Choose an audience** — start with a specific group of people (e.g., freelance designers, indie founders), not a product idea.
2. **Find their watering holes** — Reddit, forums, Twitter/X, Amazon book reviews, Slack/Discord communities, blog comments, review sites (G2, Capterra), Quora, etc.
3. **Observe and collect** — read conversations, threads, reviews. Copy/paste **exact quotes**. Don't interact, just watch.
4. **Categorize findings** — sort into: pains, jargon, desires, buying triggers, worldview, recommendations, spending patterns.
5. **Identify patterns** — one complaint is an anecdote; the same complaint across dozens of people and multiple sources is a business opportunity.

## What to Look For

- **Pain points**: "I hate when...", "I can't figure out...", "The worst part is..."
- **Exact language**: the specific words the audience uses (this becomes your marketing copy).
- **Buying triggers**: what pushes someone from "I have a problem" to "I need to fix this NOW" (deadlines, a tool breaking, boss pressure).
- **Gaps in existing solutions**: what they've tried, what disappointed them.
- **Worldview & identity**: how they see themselves, what they value, what they aspire to.
- **Spending patterns**: what they already pay for, what price ranges are normal to them.

## How Findings Are Used

- **Product development** — build things that solve verified, recurring pains.
- **Copywriting** — mirror their exact language so they feel "this was made for me."
- **Content marketing** — Amy Hoy calls these "Ebombs" (educational bombs) — free high-value content that attracts your audience.
- **Email sequences** — structure nurture sequences around identified pains, desires, and buying triggers.
- **Positioning** — differentiate against what people dislike about current solutions.

## Key Principles

1. **Audience first, product second.** Never start with a product idea. Start by deeply understanding a group of people.
2. **Observe, don't ask.** People reveal far more in natural conversation than in surveys or interviews.
3. **Collect exact quotes.** Their words are your future marketing copy.
4. **Patterns over one-offs.** A single complaint is noise. Repeated complaints across multiple sources are signal.
5. **It's ongoing, not one-time.** Sales Safari is a habit you maintain, not a phase you complete.
6. **Ship small, ship fast.** Use research to create small, focused products (start with educational content) rather than building a massive product on assumptions.

## Sources to Mine

| Source Type | Examples |
|---|---|
| Forums & Communities | Reddit, Hacker News, Stack Overflow, niche industry forums, Discourse |
| Social Media | Twitter/X threads, LinkedIn posts/comments, Facebook Groups |
| Q&A Sites | Quora, industry-specific Q&A platforms |
| Review Sites | Amazon reviews (especially books/tools), G2, Capterra, Trustpilot, app store reviews |
| Blog Comments | Comments on popular industry blogs and publications |
| Slack/Discord | Professional and industry-specific servers and workspaces |
| Conference Talks | YouTube comments on conference talks, event discussion threads |
